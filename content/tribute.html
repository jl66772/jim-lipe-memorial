Jim was born in Salinas and grew up between the Salinas Valley and Lake Tahoe, and he made his home in Salinas and Tahoma, California. He built his working life in trucking and agriculture. He founded LipeLine Trucking, and in 2007 he joined his father and brother in buying NH3 Service Company, the family business his great-grandfather Ralph Iliff started in 1938. Jim led it as president and CEO until it was sold in 2015, and carrying on his family's legacy in the valley meant a great deal to him.

Jim lived for adventure: skiing, mountain biking, and anything with a motor. He went heli-skiing in Alaska, took snowmobile treks through Desolation Wilderness, and rode dirt bikes across Baja. He lived, as his family put it, "at full throttle," and he loved the same way. After his daughter Katherine was diagnosed with type 1 diabetes, he became a passionate advocate and fundraiser for Breakthrough T1D (formerly JDRF).

Jim is survived by his children Katherine, Stephen, and James, along with his sister Julie and his brothers William and Michael, as well as extended family. He was preceded in death by his parents, Jim Sr. and Penny.
